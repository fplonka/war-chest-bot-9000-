# v5_lane_whale_25m_learning

We were trying a longer learning and steady-state check: five minutes of Greedy value-network warmup followed by 25 minutes of ReBeL, with a checkpoint every six minutes. The questions were whether games actually finish, whether throughput settles after the initial pool ages, and whether successive checkpoints become stronger in a ladder.

The run did not finish. At 985 seconds overall, 684 seconds into ReBeL, a late solve needed an 8 GiB power-of-two arena while its card was already using about 18.7 GiB. CUDA returned out of memory; Python reported the failed wave and then exited with a segmentation fault during cleanup. This is a valid-job routing bug, not a dropped-game result, and it must be fixed before another long run. The card-local whale route handled the common 4 GiB tail but was not sufficient for this rarer 8 GiB tail.

Before the failure, ReBeL completed and trained 559,104 solves with no optimizer debt, no exact CPU fallbacks, and no reported drops. Its cumulative balanced rate began at 1,329 solves/s after ten seconds, was 920/s after about six minutes, and fell to 817/s by the crash. The latest chunks were roughly 600--800 solves/s as their average configuration count rose from about 15,000 to 32,000 per optimizer batch. Games did not take twelve minutes to begin ending: the first completion appeared about 72 seconds after the ReBeL transition, and 6,670 games had finished by the crash. There were 4,398 solver node-cap fallbacks and 824 lane-isolated whale routes. Approximate ReBeL-phase average utilization was 77% on GPU 0 and 78% on GPU 1; peak memory was 21,889 MiB and 22,829 MiB.

The project at this point used the lane-local whale scheduler from `8a32c46`, not the later local transfer, buffer-retention, or fast-GEMM experiments. The run saved only the warm/init checkpoint and the six-minute checkpoint, so they can still provide a small learning sanity check, but they cannot answer the intended 25-minute ladder question.
