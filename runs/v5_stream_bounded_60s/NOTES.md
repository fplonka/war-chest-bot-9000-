# Bounded-submission trainer check

We were testing the real device-batch trainer after separating the 4,608-game state pool from GPU queue depth. Each of 36 builders could keep 128 game actors but submit at most 32 solves. A generation-only check had retained 1,458 solves/s and drained in 2.16 seconds, so this one-minute run reserved five seconds for drain.

The bound preserved live training throughput early: cumulative balanced rate was 1,261/s at 11 seconds and 1,364/s at 31 seconds, with zero debt. The second interval briefly read 1,184/s because batches averaged 107,317 configurations, then recovered. After the horizon payoff reached zero on this compressed one-minute schedule, expensive late positions appeared: large-job routes rose to 13 and true 200,000-node build caps to 48. Cumulative throughput fell to about 1,002/s by 51 seconds. Stopping at 55 seconds still took until 64.4 seconds because the bounded outstanding set now contained many tail jobs; the process overran by 4.44 seconds.

The final accounting was 53,353 solves, 212,992 optimizer rows, and 420 rows of debt, or 826.5 balanced solves/s over the actual interval. There were zero exact CPU fallbacks and zero drops. The submission bound solved ordinary queue drain but not a queue dominated by exclusive and node-capped late positions. At this point the trainer itself was fast enough; the next decisions were to measure/tune the tail route and to use the required five-minute schedule, since a one-minute run compresses cap annealing and both learning-rate decays into its first 40 seconds and is not representative of the golden run's workload evolution.
